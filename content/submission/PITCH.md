# 2-minute pitch

*Spoken, about 300 words (~150 words per minute; trim the 0:50 paragraph if you run long). Lines in brackets are stage directions.*

---

[0:00] Imagine you are eight years old, and someone tells you people will live on Mars. Sounds
easy, right? Just build a house.

But on Mars the air is mostly carbon dioxide. Water has to be recycled, almost every drop. Dust
storms can block the Sun for days. And when the Sun throws a flare, radiation reaches the ground,
because Mars has thin air and no magnetic shield around the whole planet.

[0:25] We are team hackteamVLDK, and this is **ARES-KIDS: The Jezero Signal**, a base-builder for
kids aged seven to twelve. You land in Jezero Crater, the real place where NASA's Perseverance is
exploring. Your underground base is only half built, and the builder drones are asleep. You wake
them, keep your crew healthy, and follow a mysterious signal to a canyon next to rocks like the
real "leopard spots" NASA found in 2024.

[0:50] Your guide is ASTRA 6.7, the station AI. Every time something happens, she explains the real
science in one or two short sentences: how MOXIE made oxygen on Mars, why the space station
recycles 98% of its water, why soil above your rooms blocks most radiation.

[1:10] What have we built? A working simulation engine: air, water, food, power, and radiation
dose room by room, with real 2024 solar storm dates from NASA's DONKI database. It is covered by
automated tests. The browser game and the art are next.

[1:25] And we took the science seriously. We checked seventy-five science claims in our script
against NASA and peer-reviewed sources we actually opened. Sixteen needed fixing, and we fixed
them. Every number in the game is labelled: NASA data, approximation, or game value. Kids learn
the science, and they learn to ask "how do we know?"

[1:50] Nobody dies in ARES-KIDS. When things go wrong, you learn why. Thank you!
